# Two decisions, two case studies

All findings below refer to synthetic data. They demonstrate analysis and communication, not live advertising results.

## Campaign performance: LingoLoop

**Question:** Where should a platform specialist investigate when an account average looks efficient?

The latest 30-day aggregate CPA is below the fictional $7 target. Campaign themes have different roles and very different costs. The discovery term `free spanish translation` has poor conversion despite meaningful spend. A language-learning app may not satisfy translation intent, so query relevance is the first question before a bid or budget action.

The workbench connects these observations to a transparent evidence dialog, a next step, and explicit measurement limits. It treats a tiny apparently cheap sample cautiously. It also flags substantial spend with few or no installs for investigation rather than using small install counts as a reason to continue spending blindly.

The budget model holds spend constant and transfers a capped share of Competitor spend to Category. Additional traffic uses a user-controlled marginal cost assumption. The output is a sensitivity result. No auction response, demand guarantee, or incremental lift is claimed.

**Outputs:** interactive analysis, filtered CSV, formula-based Excel workbook, and a one-page optimization brief.

## Advertiser growth: Stride and the account portfolio

**Question:** Which client conversation should happen next, and what evidence should shape it?

Eight accounts have different goals, targets, trends, and account notes. The latest 30 days are compared with the prior non-overlapping 30 days. An explicit quality concern takes priority over otherwise favorable installation CPA. Inefficient accounts are reviewed before expansion opportunities, and the sparse Trail account is held for evidence.

Stride's client note raises a subscriber-quality concern. The correct response is to request comparable retention, trial-to-paid conversion, and payback cohorts before recommending more spend. The project deliberately leaves unavailable business-value measures unavailable.

Each advertiser has a five-slide review: objective, performance, diagnosis, 30-day plan, and client questions. The plan assigns responsibilities across the client, platform specialist, client partner, and measurement team. Final evaluation can extend beyond 30 days while cohorts mature.

**Outputs:** advertiser prioritization, client reviews for all eight accounts, downloadable Markdown briefs, a five-page Stride PDF, and an Excel account-analysis workbook.
